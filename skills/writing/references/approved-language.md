# Approved language

Use approved language to preserve an attributable voice without inventing one.

## Record

Each item carries:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `text` | Exact phrase, story, opinion, lesson, or example |
| `owner` | Named person or institution that said or approved it |
| `source` | Recording, transcript, document, message, or publication |
| `capturedAt` | Date the source was created or retrieved |
| `authority` | `fact`, `interpretation`, `thesis`, `experience`, or `style` |
| `audienceScope` | Where the material may be reused |
| `permission` | `public`, `internal`, `explicit-reuse`, `restricted`, or `unknown` |
| `expiresAt` | Date or condition that makes it stale, or `null` |
| `contradicts` | Conflicting approved language that must remain visible |
| `uses` | Known publications or prompts that reused the item |

## Rules

- Preserve exact wording when the phrase itself carries voice.
- Paraphrase facts only within their evidence strength.
- Never convert an internal or restricted statement into public copy.
- Never write in a named person's first person from third-party summaries.
- Treat contradictions as a decision, not material to blend.
- Reuse does not imply currentness. Check dates and conditions before publication.
- Record the source of a story or example even when the details feel mundane.

## AI use

AI may find patterns, propose combinations, and adapt approved language to a requested format. The owner still controls the position and the final public wording. A model-generated phrase becomes approved language only after a named owner accepts it.
